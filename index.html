SHEET 01

Programmable DC Load Test Bench

Built with KiCAD, Arduino IDE, and soldering station. Tested with multimeters and LTSpice simulation.

A programmable DC electronic load for validating power source behavior under controlled load conditions. An op-amp feedback loop compares a reference voltage from an ESP32-driven DAC against the voltage across a 1Ω sense resistor, adjusting a MOSFET gate to regulate current draw. This allows rated voltage, current, and power output to be verified before a supply is trusted in a larger system.

  • Regulated load current within ~2% of setpoint across a 0.05–0.30 A sweep, verified against a bench multimeter
  • Verified the feedback loop and MOSFET gate drive in LTSpice before committing the design to hardware

SHEET 02

High-Speed Digital Circuit Demonstration PCB

Built with Altium Designer. Tested with an oscilloscope and spectrum analyzer.

A 2-layer PCB designed and built with teammates Jon Elwood and Jake Pong to make high-speed signal-integrity phenomena directly observable rather than just theoretical: ground bounce, channel loss, and near/far-end crosstalk (NEXT/FEXT). SMA-terminated test structures vary trace spacing, routing length, and geometry so each effect can be isolated and measured on its own.

  • Measured NEXT/FEXT at 3 MHz across three trace geometries, finding that tripling spacing cut coupling roughly 5×
  • Characterized ground bounce and channel loss with oscilloscope, spectrum-analyzer, and TDR measurements

SHEET 03

Audio Amplifier PCB

Built with OrCAD Capture CIS, Allegro PCB Designer, and GerbTool.

My first PCB, a deliberate, ground-up exercise in production PCB design in Cadence Allegro, taken from an existing schematic (a two-channel LM324/LM386 audio amplifier designed by Professor Stephen Petersen, UCSC) all the way to a fabricated board. The focus was mastering the full Allegro workflow: custom footprints and heat sink pads to board-house spec, through-hole and drill customization, dynamic/static copper shapes, layer management, drill legends, and Gerber verification.

  • Built board-house-compliant custom footprints, heat sink pads, and through-hole pads directly from datasheet specs
  • Generated and verified the full drill file, drill legend, and Gerber output set in GerbTool before fabrication

Basys3 VGA Game

Built with Verilog, Basys 3 FPGA. Write-up in progress.

A simplified Subway Surfers-style endless runner built entirely on a Basys 3 FPGA, using behavioral Verilog and state-machine logic in place of a microcontroller. The design generates 60 FPS VGA output from scratch, driving player movement, scrolling tracks, and multi-color obstacles directly through pixel-level graphics logic.

  • Generated randomized obstacles across three tracks with pixel-based collision detection and jump timing
  • Controlled gameplay entirely through FPGA buttons and switches, with reset control and an optional invincibility mode
