Audio Amplifier PCB

My first PCB, a deliberate, ground-up exercise in production PCB design in Cadence Allegro, taken from an existing schematic all the way to a fabricated, board-house-ready board.

Schematic By Prof. Stephen Petersen (UCSC)
Built With OrCAD Capture CIS, Allegro PCB Designer, GerbTool
ICs LM324, LM386
01

Overview

This project wasn't about circuit design: the schematic, a two-channel LM324/LM386 audio amplifier, was designed by Professor Stephen Petersen at UCSC, and I'm crediting him for it directly. What this project was about was everything downstream of a schematic: taking that circuit and producing an actual, manufacturable, fabricated board in Cadence Allegro. It was my first PCB, and I used it deliberately as a full pass through the production PCB design workflow rather than a from-scratch design exercise, so I could build real fluency in the tools before applying that fluency to boards of my own design.

02

From Schematic to Layout

Starting from the OrCAD Capture CIS schematic, I brought the design into Allegro PCB Designer and worked through the full layout process myself, translating the schematic's netlist, datasheets, and component requirements into a physical, two-layer board.

03

Allegro & PCB Design Skills Demonstrated

The point of this project was depth, not novelty, going deliberately deep into the parts of PCB design that only show up once you take a board seriously toward fabrication:

Footprints & Pads
  • Created custom footprints from datasheet package dimensions, built to the fabricating board house's specific manufacturing tolerances
  • Edited pads for drill holes and footprints individually, including through-hole customization (size, type, and placement per component)
  • Designed heat sink pads for power-dissipating components
Copper & Routing
  • Worked with both dynamic and static copper shapes, understanding when each is appropriate
  • Routed signal and power traces with attention to width, spacing, and current-carrying requirements
Layout & Libraries
  • Built the full board layout across layers in Allegro PCB Designer
  • Organized and maintained footprint libraries for reuse and consistency
  • Defined board sizing, outlines, and cut marks for fabrication
Fabrication Outputs
  • Edited drill files directly, and created a custom drill legend with drill symbols for the fabricator
  • Generated the full Gerber output set and verified every layer in GerbTool before releasing the design for manufacturing
04

Result

The board was fabricated successfully and assembled (Fig. 1), a working, physical confirmation that the footprints, drill data, and Gerber package were correct end to end. More importantly, it meant the first time I took a design through Allegro to a board house, I understood every step of what I was submitting, rather than treating fabrication as a black box.
